Interpretation and next steps · Product group A + B · 03 / 03

What the data can and cannot explain

The supplied figures show what changed: realized price fell for both products, and the sales mix moved toward B. They do not show why realized prices fell. No list prices, discount records, contracts, or policy evidence were supplied, so a claim about discounting or a pricing decision would be speculation.

The supplied totals also do not explain why the product mix changed. The bridge measures contributions to the margin movement; it does not identify commercial causes.

Method and scope

Sales = quantity × realized unit price; COGS = quantity × unit COGS; group margin = (total sales − total COGS) / total sales. Product margins are sales-weighted through group totals.

G(p,q) = 1 − Σ(qᵢcᵢ) / Σ(qᵢpᵢ)
Price = G(p₁,q₀) − G(p₀,q₀)
Mix / quantity = G(p₀,q₁) − G(p₀,q₀)
Shared = G(p₁,q₁) − G(p₁,q₀) − G(p₀,q₁) + G(p₀,q₀)

The two-factor method uses the supplied unchanged unit costs. The calculation order does not represent the order of business events. Supplied facts describe this synthetic case; they are not validated company policy.
