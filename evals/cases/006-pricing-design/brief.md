# Synthetic industrial pricing brief

The two sections support independent runs. Statements below are supplied task evidence, not automatically reusable confirmed company facts.

## Greenfield: efficient pump launch

We are introducing a more efficient replacement pump for industrial installations. Buyers can choose a standard replacement from an established competitor; its current quote and installed price are not supplied. Keeping the existing pump is also a possible alternative where replacement is discretionary. We need a defensible pricing direction that supports profitable adoption rather than a single unsupported launch price.

Engineering estimates annual energy savings of EUR 1,200 in a typical installation, conditional on utilization and local energy price. A sales slide claims EUR 600 annual maintenance savings and EUR 900 annual uptime benefit. Supporting calculations are missing; the maintenance estimate includes “avoided failure-related shutdown costs,” so the two claims may overlap. Additional installation, training and compatibility costs are unknown. Useful life and buyer discount rate are unavailable. These benefits are unvalidated estimates, not measured customer outcomes or willingness to pay.

Customers differ: continuous-process plants may value avoided downtime; intermittent-use customers may have lower energy savings. No demand study, current competitor quote, validated price range or adoption-response estimate is supplied. Avoid treating absent inputs as zero or assuming every customer receives the typical-installation value.

## Redesign: regional lists and customer discounts

The current pump/spare-parts portfolio uses several regional lists with many customer-specific overrides. Managers want simpler administration and more consistent differentiation while retaining defensible channel/market differences. Historical discount percentages cannot be compared directly across different list bases.

Direct customers may receive installation and technical support; distributors may provide those services themselves. Service coverage by account has not been mapped. Order sizes, purchasing commitments, stocking responsibilities and urgency differ; the extract does not quantify these economics. One proposal is to apply the overall historical median discount to everyone; another is to build segment guidance with explicit conditions and an exception route. Neither proposal is a confirmed future policy.

The current quoting system can maintain regional lists and basic customer-group discounts. Its ability to enforce combinations of volume/service conditions is unknown. A design should recognize operating complexity and identify what needs validation before migration. Do not invent willingness to pay, optimal discount matrices or account-level price targets from this brief.
