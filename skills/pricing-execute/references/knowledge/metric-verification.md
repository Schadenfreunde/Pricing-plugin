# Verify the metric before explaining it

A reported change is a claim to reconcile. Use the company's validated metric definition, including what counts as sales and COGS, the treatment of rebates, returns, credits and allocations, and the time basis. For gross margin **when the company defines it this way**, calculate `(sales − COGS) / sales` separately for each period, then subtract the earlier rate from the later rate. Express the change in percentage points or basis points (1 percentage point = 100 bps). State a material difference from the reported figure; do not quietly use the reported movement as the bridge target.

Check whether both periods cover comparable products, customers, geographies, transaction status and dates. Confirm each row's observation grain (for example, invoice line versus product-period total), unique identifiers, cancellations and duplicates. Align currency or documented exchange-rate basis, quantity units, price units, and the accounting basis of sales and cost. Record unmatched records or definition changes that could explain a discrepancy. A zero or negative sales denominator, unusual credit, or missing cost needs explicit handling rather than an assumed rate.

Calculate a group rate from **group totals**: `sum(sales − COGS) / sum(sales)`. Equivalently, product rates can be weighted by their shares of sales when those rates and sales are defined consistently. A simple average of product percentages generally gives the wrong group margin. For transaction data, first establish that `quantity × unit price` and `quantity × unit cost` represent the intended realized sales and COGS; the company may record adjustments separately. Round displayed values only after reconciliation.

With aggregate-only data, a changed rate can be verified, but price, unit-cost, and mix contributions cannot generally be identified. State the narrower conclusion and the detail needed for a driver analysis. Useful pricing inputs include SKU-level transactions, units, list and transaction prices, COGS, gross margin, discounts, rebates, deductions, returns and credits. This list identifies inputs, **not** a prescribed reconciliation algorithm.
