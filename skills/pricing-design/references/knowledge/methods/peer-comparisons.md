# Compare prices among economically relevant peers

Use this reference to investigate price dispersion, unusual discounts or customer/product differences. Peer comparisons identify questions; a low price, a wide distribution or an estimated residual alone does not establish leakage, willingness to pay or an attainable price increase.

## Build a defensible comparison

Define the price level and observation grain. Align product/specification, unit, currency, time, geography, channel, service, contract terms, order size and relevant customer economics where supported. Different products' absolute prices are rarely direct peers. A regional list change can make discount percentages incomparable even when net prices are comparable. Disclose missing controls rather than treating them as identical.

Start with understandable groups and distributions when the available observations support them. Show which peers exist, coverage and the comparison basis. One observed price or a thin/heterogeneous group is weaker evidence than a consistent relevant population. Avoid invented percentiles or statistical precision. Regression or other adjustment may help with sufficient data and defensible explanatory factors, but fitted prices are historical comparators, not optimal prices. No universal model-fit or outlier cutoff proves pricing discipline.

Look at commercially meaningful combinations as well as single-variable extremes: small accounts with low margin and large discounts, or favorable terms without corresponding commitments. Establish metric definitions and relevant costs before calling a customer unprofitable; arbitrary cost allocation can manufacture that conclusion.

## Interpret and act proportionately

Check documented contracts, service/value differences, approvals and [governance](discount-governance.md) before treating a difference as an actionable opportunity. Absence of a recorded exception is not proof that none exists. Concentration under a salesperson can suggest investigation, not misconduct or a proven cause.

Distinguish confidence in the measured difference from confidence that it is commercially unjustified or can be corrected. Lead opportunity ordering with the supporting evidence; layer financial scale, feasibility and customer risk separately. Large speculative gaps do not outrank stronger evidence simply because they are large. Use a question or validation step when the comparison is weak; recommendations need a credible capture mechanism, not merely a peer target.

If illustrating monetary scale, apply an explicitly assumed comparable price to eligible prior-period quantities using [price realization](price-realization.md). Label it a conditional scenario; do not sum unresolved peer gaps as collectible revenue. Qualitative ranking remains useful without volumes or costs.
