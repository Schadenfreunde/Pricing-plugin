# Read a company-defined price waterfall

A waterfall traces a price from a starting level through signed adjustments to later levels. The direct-sales examples show list price, on-invoice discounts and surcharges, invoice price, off-invoice rebates, and net price; the advanced example continues through non-cash costs and cost of money to pocket price. These are illustrative labels. Use the company's validated level definitions, accounting signs and data mappings; include only steps relevant to the question and actually supported by its records.

For a comparison, name the exact level being compared (for example, invoice or net price) and hold product, customer or segment, period, currency, unit, and adjustment basis comparable. Reconcile each step to the next level using the company's rules. Check whether credits, returns, accruals, late rebates, freight, services or financing belong at that step, and show unknown or unmatched amounts rather than assigning them a convenient bucket. A lower realized price without list-price and adjustment detail is **not** proof that discounts increased.

Interpret a policy difference at its stated scope. A permitted exception for one customer and product does not authorize the same adjustment for another; regional, channel, date, contract and approval boundaries may matter. Compare observed amounts with the validated policy and any confirmed exception. If a note conflicts with a policy or the exception scope is uncertain, clarify it before claiming compliance or leakage. This reference does not turn an unvalidated document claim into company policy.

The source's pocket margin follows its own sequence of revenue reducers, chargeable items and variable cost of goods. It is **not automatically gross margin**: the sales denominator, included costs and adjustment boundaries may differ. Reconcile both definitions before relating a waterfall to a gross-margin-rate movement. If the question only concerns gross margin and no discount records exist, the waterfall may add no supported explanation.
