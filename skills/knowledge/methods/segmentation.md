# Pricing segmentation and enforceable differences

## Find meaningful differences

- Segment around economically meaningful differences, not clustering or historical discounts alone. Do not reproduce historical concessions as invented policy.
- Consider, where supported: buyer value and use, competition, buying power, service and cost to serve, product differentiation, channel, geography, order size, commitments and flexibility.
- Size or a low price alone proves neither willingness to pay nor a justified discount.
- Allocated costs may misstate economics.
- Distinguish observed differences, provisional explanations and confirmed rules.
- Compare defined realized prices before interpreting different historical or regional list discounts.
- Use [peers](peer-comparisons.md) to assess dispersion. Peers do not establish optimal segments or targets.

## Make segments workable

- Weigh differentiation against administration, enforcement, sales adoption and systems.

**Fences**

- A pricing fence makes a difference identifiable and enforceable. Choose observable, understandable fences usable in quoting, purchase, contract or entitlement.
- Fence through product or service scope, package or included usage, commitments, volume, flexibility or other eligibility.
- Explain each fence's economic basis and practical application.

**Risks and constraints**

- Consider arbitrage, cannibalization, customer understanding, fairness, exceptions and system capability.
- Existing contracts and confirmed exceptions constrain migration.
- Proposed floors, targets and authority remain design choices.

## Compare and validate

- Compare a few workable structures.
- Thin evidence supports candidate logic and the validation it needs, not fabricated targets or elasticity.
- A redesign needs a transition plan and comparable-outcome monitoring.
- A new strategy needs relevant buyer and value evidence.
