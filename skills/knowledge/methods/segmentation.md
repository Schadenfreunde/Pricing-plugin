# Pricing segmentation and enforceable differences

Use this reference when customers, uses, products or terms differ meaningfully enough to justify different pricing approaches. Segmentation is a pricing design choice, not merely a clustering exercise or a ranking of historical discounts.

## Choose economically relevant differences

Consider buyer value/use, competition, buying power, service/cost-to-serve, product differentiation, channel roles, geography, order size, commitments and flexibility where evidence supports them. Customer size or low observed price alone does not establish willingness to pay or a justified discount. Do not treat arbitrary allocated costs as the true economics of a segment.

Distinguish observed differences, provisional explanations and confirmed company rules. If historical price structures changed or regional list bases differ, compare the defined realized level before interpreting discount percentages. [Peer comparisons](peer-comparisons.md) can identify questions, but do not prove the intended segmentation or define optimal segment targets.

Choose dimensions that are understandable, observable and usable in quoting. Assess whether incremental differentiation is worth the administration, enforcement, sales adoption and system effort. Separate service/value differences from an invented policy that simply reproduces historical concessions.

## Make differentiation workable

A pricing fence is a condition that makes the offer/price difference identifiable and enforceable: product/service scope, purchase commitment, order volume, contract flexibility, channel or other relevant eligibility. Explain why that condition relates to value or economics and how users can apply it. A named segment with no usable eligibility or boundary may leak across offers.

Consider arbitrage/cannibalization, customer understanding, fairness perceptions, exceptions and what the quoting system can support. Existing contracts and known tolerated exceptions constrain transition. Proposed floors, targets, discount conditions and authority belong to a design until validated as company rules; no universal matrix or approval hierarchy follows from this reference.

Compare a small number of workable structures. Where evidence is thin, define candidate segment logic and the data/validation needed, rather than manufacture target prices or segment elasticity. A redesign needs a migration path and monitoring of comparable realized outcomes; a new strategy needs evidence about relevant buyers and value before assuming coverage.
