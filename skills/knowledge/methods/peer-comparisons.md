# Compare economically relevant peers

- Price dispersion, low prices and statistical residuals identify leads. They do not prove leakage, willingness to pay or attainable increases.

## Define comparable peers

- Define the price level and grain.
- Verify comparability on:
  - product and specification, or package and features
  - included units, cadence and service term, commitments
  - period, unit and currency
  - relevant geography, channel, service, contracts, order size and customer economics
- Equal normalized monthly amounts do not make unlike cancellation, service or risk terms comparable.
- Absolute prices of different products are rarely direct peers.
- Different list bases can invalidate discount comparisons even where net prices are comparable.
- Disclose missing controls.

## Analyze the distribution

- Start with understandable groups and distributions. Show actual coverage.
- Thin or heterogeneous peer groups weaken evidence. Avoid invented percentiles or precision.
- Regression needs sufficient data and defensible factors.
- Fitted historical prices are comparators, not optimal targets.
- No universal fit or outlier cutoff proves pricing discipline.
- Inspect meaningful combinations, such as low revenue with low margin, or concessions without commitments.
- Establish costs before calling a customer unprofitable. Arbitrary allocations can manufacture that result.

## Judge actionability

- Check value differences and [governance](discount-governance.md).
- Missing recorded exceptions do not prove they are absent.
- Salesperson concentration suggests investigation, not misconduct.
- Apply [evidence ranking](evidence-ranking.md) when ordering leads.

## Size the opportunity

- Use [price-change economics](price-change-economics.md) for conditional monetary sizing, with assumed comparable prices and eligible prior quantities.
- Unresolved peer gaps are not collectible revenue.
