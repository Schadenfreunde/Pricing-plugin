# Evaluate pricing experiments and promotions

Use when assessing incremental promotion, price, package, trial or paywall effects. Define the decision, population, economic outcome, comparison horizon and assignment unit before interpreting results. Apply [metric verification](../metric-verification.md) and the applicable retail/recurring recipe.

## Identify the evidence

Distinguish randomized experiments, defensible quasi-experimental estimates and descriptive comparisons. Historical price-sales correlation or a before/after comparison is not an identified demand response. State the estimator's assumptions and remaining confounds.

Check assignment, eligible population, exposure, contamination, sample balance and outcome logging. Preserve the requested estimand: an assignment-based comparison must not silently become a comparison of only purchasers, converters or surviving subscribers. Exposure-based estimates need their own justified definition. An unobserved outcome is not zero. Repeated user/account/store observations are not automatically independent samples.

Use compatible calendars and opportunity to purchase/use. Stockouts, acquisition campaigns, assortment changes, seasonality and channel changes can alter results. Substitutes, marketplace participants and shared inventory can create interference; choose or qualify the design accordingly.

## Measure economics over a useful window

Compare contribution on a matched revenue/cost basis, alongside relevant volume, conversion, acquisition and customer measures. Include applicable discount funding, stacking, fees, refunds, fulfillment and service costs once. Sales lift alone is not incremental profit.

Retail promotions can shift brand/SKU choice, pack mix and purchase timing. Assess supported pre/post windows, stockpiling and cannibalization across the economically relevant portfolio. Manufacturer shipments do not prove consumer lift or pass-through.

Trials, renewals, returns and churn have delayed outcomes. Report cohort age, observation cutoff, incomplete/censored outcomes and the scope of matured results. Early conversion cannot establish long-term retention or LTV. Use [recurring metrics](../recurring-metrics.md) for acquisition-cost/payback/lifetime recipes and lifecycle definitions.

## Decide what the result supports

Report effect size, uncertainty and practical economics where the data/design support them. Disclose multiple comparisons, peeking/stopping and selection limitations; do not invent power, confidence intervals or probabilities. A small sample can support a directional learning result without a precise optimized price.

For a proposed test, specify hypothesis, assignment/exposure, primary outcome, horizon, relevant guardrails and decision/stopping conditions. Guardrails can include contribution, refunds, retention, customer complaints, inventory and service capacity as relevant. Discount depth, profit floors and stopping thresholds require evidence or owner choice, not defaults.

State what can be concluded, what needs another cohort/window and what may change the decision. An experiment recommendation does not authorize price changes, feature-flag updates or customer communications.
