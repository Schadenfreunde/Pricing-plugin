# Evaluate pricing experiments and promotions

- Use when assessing incremental promotion, price, package, trial or paywall effects.
- Before interpreting results, define the decision, population, economic outcome, comparison horizon and assignment unit.
- Apply [metric verification](../metric-verification.md) and the applicable retail or recurring recipe.

## Identify the evidence

**Design type**

- Distinguish randomized experiments, defensible quasi-experimental estimates and descriptive comparisons.
- Historical price-sales correlation or a before/after comparison is not an identified demand response.
- State the estimator's assumptions and remaining confounds.

**Assignment and outcomes**

- Check assignment, eligible population, exposure, contamination, sample balance and outcome logging.
- Preserve the requested estimand. An assignment-based comparison must not silently become a comparison of only purchasers, converters or surviving subscribers.
- Exposure-based estimates need their own justified definition.
- Repeated user, account or store observations are not automatically independent samples.

**Context**

- Use compatible calendars and opportunity to purchase or use.
- Stockouts, acquisition campaigns, assortment changes, seasonality and channel changes can alter results.
- Substitutes, marketplace participants and shared inventory can create interference. Choose or qualify the design accordingly.

## Measure economics over a useful window

- Compare contribution on a matched revenue and cost basis, alongside relevant volume, conversion, acquisition and customer measures.
- Include applicable discount funding, stacking, fees, refunds, fulfillment and service costs once.
- Sales lift alone is not incremental profit.

**Retail promotions**

- Promotions can shift brand or SKU choice, pack mix and purchase timing.
- Assess supported pre/post windows, stockpiling and cannibalization across the economically relevant portfolio.
- Manufacturer shipments do not prove consumer lift or pass-through.

**Delayed outcomes**

- Trials, renewals, returns and churn have delayed outcomes.
- Report cohort age, observation cutoff, incomplete or censored outcomes and the scope of matured results.
- Early conversion cannot establish long-term retention or LTV.
- Use [recurring metrics](../recurring-metrics.md) for acquisition-cost, payback and lifetime recipes and lifecycle definitions.

## Decide what the result supports

**Reporting a result**

- Report effect size, uncertainty and practical economics where the data and design support them.
- Disclose multiple comparisons, peeking or early stopping, and selection limitations.
- Do not invent power, confidence intervals or probabilities.

**Proposing a test**

- Specify hypothesis, assignment and exposure, primary outcome, horizon, relevant guardrails and decision or stopping conditions.
- Guardrails can include contribution, refunds, retention, customer complaints, inventory and service capacity.
- Discount depth, profit floors and stopping thresholds need evidence or an owner's choice, not defaults.

**Conclusion**

- State what can be concluded, what needs another cohort or window, and what may change the decision.
