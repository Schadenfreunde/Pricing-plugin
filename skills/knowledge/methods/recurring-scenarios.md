# Rate and compare recurring or usage scenarios

Use when linear quantity × unit-price arithmetic does not represent the contract, or when recurring/lifecycle movements matter. Establish the metric through [recurring metrics](../recurring-metrics.md) and verify inputs with [metric verification](../metric-verification.md).

## Apply the actual rating rules

Represent the relevant price/package version, entitlement, billable quantity/usage, eligibility state and time basis. Apply fixed fees, included units, minimums, commitments/credits, add-ons, tiers, overages, discounts and proration only as supported by the contract/selected recipe.

Before delivery, reconcile inclusion/exclusion summaries against every reported result’s component lines. If a component appears in any result, do not call it globally excluded; scope exclusion claims to the specific schedule, version, period, and result.

When a material billing order is unresolved, hold the affected invoice calculation and ask for the governing sequence; give only independently supported components. Provide conditional order scenarios only when the user explicitly requests them.

Graduated tiers charge units within each band; volume tiers can apply the reached band's rate to all qualifying units. Blended average prices do not identify the same schedule. Preserve service periods, price effective dates, billing cadence and provider rounding rules. Handle duplicate, corrected and late meter events under an evidenced business-key/cutoff policy; processing time and consumption time may differ.

A snapshot cannot recover absent contract/event history. Show unsupported rating components and affected coverage rather than filling them with assumptions disguised as observed facts.

## Explain observed change

Choose a reproducible bridge over comparable records: price-version changes, seats/usage, package/entitlement, discount expiry, lifecycle eligibility, scope and FX as relevant. State counterfactuals and order. For gross-margin-rate bridges on linear comparable items with verified unchanged unit costs and only price and quantity/composition factors, keep the [margin-drivers](../margin-drivers.md) convention and its separate shared effect for recurring and industrial items alike; a sequential allocation applies only if explicitly requested and named, with its different interaction allocation shown. For a named revenue/MRR bridge involving tariffs or lifecycle state, interactions may be separate or assigned by a declared sequential convention; show any unexplained residual separately and reconcile to verified endpoints.

For a pure historical price counterfactual, rate the same prior representative quantities, entitlements and eligible state under both observed price schedules; applicable linear baskets keep the [price-realization](price-realization.md) convention regardless of known demand response. New seats or plan upgrades are not automatically price realization. Structural/package changes may prevent an equivalent-service comparison; retain that limit.

## Compare proposed cases

Name current and proposed schedules, eligible cohort, renewal/effective dates, horizon and quantity/usage assumptions. When response is unknown, hold representative prior quantities/state fixed for a conditional base, then show explicitly assumed adoption, churn or usage sensitivities separately. Do not present a monthly snapshot uplift as realized annual revenue.

Include comparable proposed costs (fulfillment, infrastructure, support, fees, hardware/service, implementation) in the principal case; cost response can differ from revenue tiers. Separate recurring contribution, recognized revenue supplied by finance, invoicing and cash timing.

Reuse [price-change economics](price-change-economics.md) for applicable linear contributions and breakeven; nonlinear schedules, delayed renewal, changing fixed costs or eligibility need scenario evaluation.

Keep gross/net retention, customer counts and service constraints visible where the choice depends on them. Use a supported finite horizon and explicit survival/response assumptions for lifetime scenarios; useful conditional cases do not establish optimal pricing or capture.
