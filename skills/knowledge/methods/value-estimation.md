# Estimate differentiated customer value

## Set the comparison

- Compare the offer with the buyer's credible next-best alternative, including existing equipment where relevant.
- Buyer value, seller economics and competition serve different purposes. None alone determines an optimal selling price.
- Establish the buyer and use case, the product or service scope and a supported price basis.
- Do not invent competitor quotes or assume one alternative fits every segment.

## Assess the differences

- Assess supported positive and negative differences: operating savings, output and quality, digital access, time and convenience, service flexibility, downtime, installation, integration, training, maintenance and adoption risk.
- Separate measured inputs, estimates and hypotheses.
- A few credible drivers beat elaborate fabricated inputs.

**Consistency**

- Align currency, unit, utilization and time horizon and timing.
- Annual savings cannot be added to a one-time equipment price without that treatment.
- Avoid overlaps, such as downtime counted again in maintenance savings.
- Consumer benefits without a supported monetary value can still be assessed qualitatively.

## From value to price

- Value creation differs from willingness to pay and from captured price. A value estimate neither compels payment nor supports a universal capture percentage. Adoption effort, purchasing constraints, risk and timing matter.
- Credible, usable differences may justify [segmentation](segmentation.md).
- Compare price and structure options against alternatives, seller economics, feasibility and customer acceptance.
- Validate important assumptions before treating an estimate as established.
- Recommendations need a plausible capture mechanism.
